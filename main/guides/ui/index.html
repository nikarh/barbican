The web UI

The UI is one page and one WebSocket (/api/ws), served by the agent on its unix socket only, and to browsers by barbican ui-proxy in a container behind your reverse proxy’s authentication. Everything it shows is live: new events, bans and edge reports arrive as they happen. It has no login of its own and is full control over the agent: see Behind a reverse proxy for who can reach it.

The screens are Overview, Addresses, Bans, Events, Rules and Settings. On a phone they are a tab bar at the bottom (without Events), which hides while you scroll. The header shows whether the UI is connected (Live); when the agent can’t be reached, a banner says so and why, and the UI reconnects on its own.

The screenshots on this site are made from synthetic data (mise run docs:screenshots).

Overview

Section titled “Overview”
  • Counters: bans in force (and how many started today), events today, addresses seen, trusted addresses, and packets dropped, on this host and on each edge.
  • Last 30 days: bans and events per day.
  • Recent bans, with their address or prefix, country, reason, when they started and how long they have left.
  • Live: events as they are read, with the address, the source, what happened and the rule that matched.
  • Firewall: whether it is applied (or in dry run, or failing, with the error), how many addresses and prefixes are blocked, and how many country and list prefixes are loaded.
  • Geolocation and Feeds: the databases and lists in use, when they were last fetched, and their errors.
  • Sources: each source’s input, how many lines it read and matched, when the last came, and its error.
  • Edges: each edge that follows the agent, connected or not, enforcing or in dry run, what it holds and dropped.
  • Banned, by country and Events, by rule, over the last 30 days.

Addresses

Section titled “Addresses”

Every address a source saw doing something that a rule matched, or logging in. The list searches by address, prefix, country code, AS1234 or network name, filters to banned or trusted addresses, and sorts by when they were last or first seen, by how active they were, or by country.

An address’s page says where it is (country, network), when it was first and last seen, and its state: banned (until when), trusted (by whom), allowed, on the deny list, in a blocked country, or listed in feeds. Activity is everything it did, grouped by what it was, the rule, and the source, with how many times and when first and last; Bans is its ban history. From here it can be banned, its ban lifted, allowed, or no longer trusted.

Events

Section titled “Events”

Every recorded event, by hour, newest first: the address and its country, the kind (HTTP request, failed or successful login, unknown user, probe), what happened (GET example.com/wp-login.php → 404, a user name, a probe’s description), the rule it was recorded under, the source, and how many times within that hour. It searches what happened, and filters by kind, rule and source. Clicking a row opens the address.

Bans

Section titled “Bans”

Bans in force (or all of them, lifted and expired too), newest first: what is blocked (an address or a prefix), its country, the reason (the rule, or the reason given by hand), when it started, and when it ends. It searches by address, prefix, country code or reason. Ban bans an address or a prefix by hand, for a length you choose.

Rules

Section titled “Rules”

The rules in the order they are tried, each with a switch, what it looks at, its threshold and ban. They can be reordered, edited, added and deleted, and deleted defaults added back. See Rules and policy.

Settings

Section titled “Settings”
  • Lengths and countries: the policy: ban lengths, repeat offenders, lengths per country, network and blocklist, countries and continents blocked as a whole, trust and event retention.
  • Allow and deny: the allow and deny lists.
  • Feeds: lists fetched from the internet.
  • Geolocation: the country and network databases: which ones are in use, from when, when they were downloaded, whether downloads are on and when the agent looks for new ones next, and download errors.
  • Edges (with the edge API on): every configured edge client and what it reports: see Edge agents.
  • About: the version, the sources, whether the firewall is on or in dry run, whether the edge API is on, and its certificate’s fingerprint, which edges are configured with.
